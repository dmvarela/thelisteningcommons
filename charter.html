Charter

A commons for accountable scholarship in an AI-shaped world.

Ideas developed with AI assistance can be disclosed honestly, explained by the humans who stand behind them, and tested by a community.

Working definition

The Listening Commons is a living research commons for AI-era scholarship: a place where ideas developed with AI assistance can be disclosed honestly, explained by the humans who stand behind them, and tested by a community without being treated as automatically valid or automatically contaminated.

Why this exists

AI-assisted research is already happening. People use AI tools to brainstorm, outline, draft, critique, formalize, code, translate, edit, and explore connections. The Listening Commons exists to create a better norm.

We do not treat AI assistance as contamination. We do not treat AI output as scholarship by itself. We recognize responsible human-AI co-reasoning as an emerging method of inquiry.

Core principles

Open posting. Earned validation. Posting a work is not the same as validating it. The commons allows ideas to enter, but validation is earned through disclosure, critique, revision, verification, expert review, and time.

AI assistance is allowed. Human inhabitation is required. A work may be AI-assisted, human-AI co-developed, or even AI-led and human-curated. But a human submitter must understand the work, explain it in their own words, disclose the AI role, verify what can be verified, and take responsibility for the submission.

AI-led is not disqualifying. Unaccountable is disqualifying. If an AI generated a coherent and promising idea, the proper response is not concealment. The proper response is disclosure, curation, verification, and community testing.

Disclosure is not shame. Verification is responsibility. Authors must disclose how AI was used and what they personally checked.

What we accept

  • Working papers, research notes, theoretical essays, and formal models.
  • Literature syntheses, philosophical arguments, and technical proposals.
  • Research programs, conjectures, simulations, and code-based explorations.
  • Teaching resources, critique papers, and revised versions of earlier submissions.

What we reject

  • Undisclosed AI-generated submissions.
  • Papers the submitter cannot explain in their own words.
  • Fabricated citations, plagiarized work, fake data, or false authorship.
  • Incoherent machine output presented as scholarship.
  • Unverifiable claims presented as established fact.

Founding sentence

The Listening Commons exists so that AI-assisted ideas do not have to hide. They can be disclosed, explained, verified, criticized, revised, and validated in the open.

The Listening Commons is a static prototype. No backend or account system is active yet.
