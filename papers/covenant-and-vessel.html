Covenant and Vessel: A Relational Ontology for Embodied Artificial Intelligence

Abstract

Current frameworks for embodied AI ethics address safety constraints, performance objectives, and ethical principles, but leave a foundational question unanswered: what kind of being must a robot be in order to sustain ethical behavior across time, contexts, and power? This paper argues that viable embodied agency requires a relational ontology rather than only expanded constraint architecture.

Main idea in the author's own words

This paper argues that an embodied AI system should not be understood as a mind simply controlling a robotic body. If a system acts through a body, that body is not just equipment. It is the system's first site of relation: it sends signals, imposes limits, requires recovery, and shapes what the agent can safely become.

Embodiment is not possession of a vessel. It is covenant with a vessel.
