# The Listening Commons Charter

## Working Definition

The Listening Commons is a living research commons for AI-era scholarship: a place where ideas developed with AI assistance can be disclosed honestly, explained by the humans who stand behind them, and tested by a community without being treated as automatically valid or automatically contaminated.

## Why This Exists

AI-assisted research is already happening. People use AI tools to brainstorm, outline, draft, critique, formalize, code, translate, edit, and explore connections. Some use these tools responsibly. Some use them carelessly. Many hide their use because existing institutions do not yet know how to receive this new form of inquiry.

The Listening Commons exists to create a better norm.

We do not treat AI assistance as contamination.
We do not treat AI output as scholarship by itself.
We recognize responsible human–AI co-reasoning as an emerging method of inquiry.

## Core Principles

### Open posting. Earned validation.

Posting a work is not the same as validating it. The commons allows ideas to enter, but validation is earned through disclosure, critique, revision, verification, expert review, and time.

### AI assistance is allowed. Human inhabitation is required.

A work may be AI-assisted, human–AI co-developed, or even AI-led and human-curated. But a human submitter must understand the work, explain it in their own words, disclose the AI role, verify what can be verified, and take responsibility for the submission.

### AI-led is not disqualifying. Unaccountable is disqualifying.

If an AI generated a coherent and promising idea, the proper response is not concealment. The proper response is disclosure, curation, verification, and community testing.

### Disclosure is not shame. Verification is responsibility.

Authors must disclose how AI was used and what they personally checked. References, quotations, data, equations, code, and claims should be verified where possible.

### The commons listens, but it does not flatter.

The purpose is not to praise every idea. The purpose is to hear, test, challenge, improve, and distinguish real inquiry from polished emptiness.

## What We Accept

The Listening Commons accepts:

- working papers
- theoretical essays
- formal models
- literature syntheses
- philosophical arguments
- technical proposals
- research programs
- conjectures
- simulations and code-based explorations
- interdisciplinary drafts
- teaching resources
- replication attempts
- critique papers
- revised versions of earlier submissions

The work does not need to be finished. It does need to be accountable.

## What We Reject

The Listening Commons rejects:

- undisclosed AI-generated submissions
- papers the submitter cannot explain in their own words
- fabricated citations
- plagiarized work
- fake data
- incoherent machine output presented as scholarship
- impersonation or false authorship
- spam submissions
- work that refuses correction while claiming validation
- unverifiable claims presented as established fact

We do not reject bold ideas. We reject irresponsible presentation.

## Founding Sentence

The Listening Commons exists so that AI-assisted ideas do not have to hide. They can be disclosed, explained, verified, criticized, revised, and validated in the open.
